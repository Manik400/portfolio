WHAT CHANGED AFTER I TOUCHED IT.

Every line below is a before and an after on a system with real users on it. No estimates.

By Manik Goyal · Software Engineer · Backend & Distributed Systems · Gurugram

I work on a video analytics platform watching 400+ cameras across 200+ servers — ANPR, face recognition and intrusion events, all of it arriving faster than any single service wants to handle it. My job is making that traffic land correctly: event-specific Kafka topics with dead-letter handling, TimescaleDB that ingests instead of choking, exports that stream instead of dying.

Two years at I2V Systems, .NET 8 and Angular/React daily, with the C++ and FFmpeg work when the problem drops below the service layer.

Split one overloaded pipeline into event-specific topics with dead-letter handling, so a poisoned ANPR message stops blocking face and intrusion events instead of stalling all three.

Rebuilt the export around streaming reads and incremental batches. The out-of-memory failures on large ranges stopped with it.

TimescaleDB hypertables with native compression, retention policies and composite indexes — sized for what 400 cameras actually push, not what a demo pushes.

Face enrollment, watchlist matching and deduplication on Milvus / pgvector — re-tuned the index so it fits the box, with match accuracy held.

Optimistic-concurrency booking safeguards and an idempotent credit ledger that hold under overlapping requests — the interesting half of the problem. Source · Live.
